01 / RESEARCH

Small systems
Fundamental questions

From the limits of classical correlations to faster thermalization and more efficient control, I study what quantum systems can do, and how to make them do it.

01Quantum thermodynamics How can we control quantum dynamics and prepare useful states?

I study quantum heat engines, thermalization, state preparation, and monitored transport. Geometric and spectral methods connect thermodynamic performance with the control of quantum dynamics.

Quantum heat engines · Thermalization · Quantum controlRelated works
02Finite-time and stochastic thermodynamics What sets the cost and fluctuations of fast processes?

Thermodynamic geometry, optimal control, and fluctuation relations reveal the energetic cost of finite-time processes, from Brownian heat engines and membrane separation to quantum work and heat statistics.

Thermodynamic geometry · Optimal control · FluctuationsRelated works
03Quantum nonlocality and many-body systems How can we prepare and certify quantum many-body states?

I use symmetry, spectral-gap optimization, and Lindblad dynamics to prepare and control many-body states. Multipartite Bell inequalities provide tests and certification of correlations beyond classical physics.

Bell inequalities · Spectral gaps · Lindblad dynamicsRelated works

03 / ABOUT ME

A geometric perspective
on the quantum world

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Leiden University’s Lorentz Institute, working with Jordi Tura. My research connects quantum information, nonequilibrium statistical physics, and optimal control.

My path began with physics and mathematics at Peking University, followed by a Ph.D. at the Beijing Computational Science Research Center and postdoctoral research with H. T. Quan at Peking University.
