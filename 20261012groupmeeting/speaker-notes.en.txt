Recursive Facet Expansion of Bell Polytopes

23 minutes: robustness selects facets; DD motivates recursive pivots; the method builds four-party partial libraries and violation landscapes. 7 minutes: a separate discussion of shared-strategy resources and SDP certificates.

01  0:00–0:30  Recursive Facet Expansion of Bell Polytopes
The story starts with a physical objective: finding Bell tests that tolerate more uniform noise. Our updated first paper shows that searching facets loses no robustness optimum. Direct Double Description supplies facets but can leave large boundary problems unfinished. We therefore explore their adjacency with recursive pivots and apply this to two four-party correlator projections. After the original landscape results, a separate discussion asks about shared-strategy resources and future SDP certificates.

02  0:30–1:55  Robust Bell-test search reduces to facet search
Speaker script (about 90 seconds):
Which Bell test tolerates the most uniform noise? Set the uniform behavior’s score to zero. Then the quantum-to-classical ratio is \(\Delta=\beta_Q/\beta_C\), and a violating strategy has critical visibility \(v_c=1/\Delta\). Larger ratios mean greater noise tolerance.

Our updated first paper shows that restricting this search to facets loses no optimum. A nondegenerate local ratio maximum defines a facet. A nonfacet local maximum has flat coefficient directions along which we can tighten the inequality without decreasing the ratio. The figure shows new classical vertices becoming active until a facet is reached.

This gives us the target for the pivot project. We want to discover and connect facet Bell tests in four-party projections. The next issue is computational: how do we explore that boundary when direct conversion is unfinished?

Reference, outside the timed script:
Fix the quantum strategy domain and remove normalization/nonsignaling coefficient redundancies. Set \(I(P_{\mathrm{uni}})=0\). The compact coefficient polytope is \(K=\{T:T\cdot v\geq-1\ \forall v\in V\}\); the scale-normalized search lies on its boundary \(\partial K=\{T:\beta_C(T)=-1\}\). Since \(-\beta_Q\) is convex, decomposing a point of \(K\) into its extreme points proves the displayed optimal-value equality. The extreme points correspond to facets. For a restricted coefficient subspace these are projection facets. A fixed variational family is allowed if its true infimum is used; individual numerical see-saw scores do not certify it. The statement does not guarantee that a numerical optimizer reaches the global maximum.

03  1:55–3:20  Direct DD leaves difficult boundary problems
Speaker script (about 90 seconds):
We now know that facets are sufficient. Double Description, or DD, converts local vertices into supporting facet inequalities. But a short vertex list can have a very large boundary. The full \((4,2,2)\) scenario already has dimension 80, despite only 256 deterministic behaviors.

Our method starts from one facet and pivots across its ridges to neighboring facets. DD still solves manageable face boundaries. When such a call remains unfinished, recursive expansion enters that face as a lower-dimensional child and explores its boundary progressively. Verified child facets supply parent ridges and pivots, while unresolved work remains recorded.

We apply this to the 32-dimensional one-/two-body projection and the 40-dimensional two-/four-body projection. Both libraries remain partial. The next pages explain exactly what a facet, a ridge, and one pivot mean.

Reference, outside the timed script:
Four parties fix two binary outputs each, so \(n_V=2^8=256\). Each party is absent from a correlator or contributes one of two settings, giving \(D_{\mathrm{full}}=3^4-1=80\). The one-/two-body projection has 256 vertices. The even-order two-/four-body projection identifies global output reversals and has 128 distinct projected vertices. Neither projection imposes PI coefficients. The v0 libraries have 2,827,535 and 7,573,123 classes, with 99 and 80 unresolved root owners; these open tasks demonstrate remaining boundary difficulty, without establishing a speed comparison with full DD. DD remains part of the method. Recursion does not remove enumeration complexity or guarantee a speedup. Double-precision cddlib candidate checks do not alone prove complete enumeration; page nine states the required exact closure hypotheses.

04  3:20–4:10  Facet, ridge, and the graph of Bell tests
Start with deterministic local behaviors \(V\), whose convex hull is the local polytope \(\mathcal L\). The dimension \(D\) is its affine dimension in the chosen coordinates. We use the lower-bound convention: \(I_F(p)\geq\beta_C\) for all local \(p\).

The tight set \(F=\{p\in\mathcal L:I_F(p)=\beta_C\}\) is a facet only if its dimension is \(D-1\). A valid inequality with a smaller tight set is not a facet. A ridge has dimension \(D-2\). In three dimensions, a facet is a polygonal face and a ridge is an edge. Every ridge of a convex polytope belongs to exactly two facets, including for a degenerate polytope.

The facet graph therefore has a node for each facet and an edge for each ridge adjacency. These graph nodes are different from the deterministic vertices in \(V\). Later we quotient graph nodes by relabeling symmetry. The question is now concrete: given \(F\) and one ridge of \(F\), how do we obtain the other facet?

06  5:30–7:05  One pivot keeps a ridge and reaches the next facet
The current facet is \(F=\{p\in\mathcal L:I_F(p)=\beta_C\}\), and we select one of its ridges \(R\). Here \(J\) is an affine supporting function of a facet of the child polytope \(F\): it is nonnegative on \(F\), and its zero set within \(F\) is exactly \(R\). It is not the quantum objective.

Extend \(J\) to the ambient affine space so that at least one outside vertex has \(J(v)<0\). This is not a claim that \(J\geq0\) on the whole local polytope. Such an extension can be obtained by subtracting a sufficiently large multiple of \(I_F-\beta_C\), which vanishes on \(F\). For \(I_t=I_F+tJ\), points on \(R\) remain tight, and points on \(F\setminus R\) move away from equality.

For an outside vertex, \(I_t(v)-\beta_C=[I_F(v)-\beta_C]+tJ(v)\). If \(J(v)<0\), its local constraint remains valid only up to \(t\leq[I_F(v)-\beta_C]/[-J(v)]\). The numerator is its initial slack; the denominator is the slack loss per unit coefficient step. The minimum of these upper limits is \(t_*\), the largest step preserving all local constraints. For example, limits \(6/3=2\) and \(5/1=5\) give \(t_*=2\).

At that step, include every outside vertex attaining the minimum, including ties. Together with the ridge they span a face of dimension \(D-1\), which is the unique other facet containing \(R\). Verify support and affine rank exactly. The step parameter is not a probability, physical time, or a specified Euclidean angle. Changing the extension of \(J\) can reparameterize the same geometric move. After pivoting, recenter the uniform behavior before comparing quantum-violation ratios. Pivot alone does not guarantee a better quantum ratio.

07  7:05–8:35  Finding a ridge is a smaller facet problem
Page six assumes that a ridge and its supporting function \(J\) are known. This page explains where they come from. The tight deterministic vertices \(V_F\) define the child polytope \(P_F=F\), viewed in its own affine space of dimension \(D-1\). Its facets have dimension \(D-2\), so they are precisely the parent ridges contained in \(F\).

In the CHSH drawing, a triangular facet is a two-dimensional child and its three edges are its child facets. In the 40-dimensional four-party projection, a facet becomes a 39-dimensional child and its facets have dimension 38. Enumerating those child facets supplies both the ridge vertices and the function \(J\) needed for the parent pivot.

For a small child, direct Double Description can convert vertices to a complete facet list. If that boundary problem is too large, apply the same facet-neighbor expansion to the child; if needed, descend again to its own facets. Each descent lowers the affine dimension by one. A certified child facet gives one certified parent ridge even if other branches remain unfinished. The recursion does not by itself guarantee feasible runtime or global completion. Once all branches of a facet are closed, its full ridge list is available.

08  8:35–9:40  Recursive expansion records what is still open
Double Description, abbreviated DD, is a direct conversion between vertex and inequality descriptions. U denotes a task pending direct DD; N denotes one pending recursive expansion; Z is computational ridge closure; A is unresolved at the recursion-depth limit \(\ell\). These labels describe computational completion, not different physical resources. The numerical implementation uses double-precision cddlib. Certified completeness additionally requires exact or independently validated boundary enumeration. Checking each returned candidate alone does not prove that DD omitted no facets.

Global relabeling symmetry groups equivalent facets, and canonicalization stores one representative per orbit. At the child level use the stabilizer of the parent facet, namely relabelings that leave that facet invariant. A completed child boundary is lifted back to supply parent ridges and pivots. A partial task can publish independently verified discoveries while retaining an unresolved frontier. Persistent task records allow the work to resume after interruption. To claim complete enumeration, every required boundary task must be closed.

09  9:40–10:40  Complete enumeration requires a closed boundary
This is a conditional geometric completeness statement. Start from a nonempty set of exactly validated facets. If every stored facet has all of its ridges, and every exact pivot across each ridge returns a represented facet class, no graph edge leaves the set. The full facet graph of a convex polytope is connected, so the set must be the whole graph, up to the stated relabeling symmetry. Support, dimension, and complete child boundaries are essential hypotheses. Stable counts, coverage of stored classes, and numerical quantum values are not substitutes for these hypotheses. The four-party libraries later in the talk retain open tasks and do not satisfy this global certificate.

12  13:00–14:10  Quantum violation supplies a height on the graph
Use the lower-bound convention \(I_F(P_{\mathrm{uni}})=0\), \(\beta_{C,F}<0\). Let \(\widehat\beta_{Q,F}\) be the score of a verified attained quantum strategy, and \(\beta_{Q,F}\) the true quantum minimum. Then \(\beta_{Q,F}\leq\widehat\beta_{Q,F}\), so \(\widehat\Delta_F=\widehat\beta_{Q,F}/\beta_{C,F}\leq\Delta_F\).

For an attained violation \(\widehat\beta_{Q,F}<\beta_{C,F}<0\), and \(0\leq v\leq1\), uniform-behavior noise \(P_v=vP+(1-v)P_{\mathrm{uni}}\) gives \(I_F(P_v)=v\widehat\beta_{Q,F}\). Strict violation occurs exactly when \(v>1/\widehat\Delta_F\). A larger attained ratio therefore demonstrates a lower visibility threshold for that particular optimized test. The optimum threshold obeys \(v_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{opt}}\leq1/\widehat\Delta_F\). Comparing attained heights does not certify the ordering of true quantum optima.

Uniform behavior is uniform output probability for every setting. It coincides with maximally mixed state noise only for measurements that map the maximally mixed state to that uniform behavior, as for traceless binary qubit observables. Each facet uses its own independently optimized state and measurements; no shared physical implementation is imposed.

17  19:10–20:25  A pivot path follows increasing recorded violation
Remove self-loops and include classes with recorded \(\widehat\Delta>1+10^{-9}\). Here \(\mathcal N(F)\) is the recorded neighboring-class set. At each step look up their already optimized heights and choose the largest strictly improving value, with tolerance \(10^{-9}\). If none exists, stop. A basin is the set of starting classes ending at one stopping class under this rule.

Strict increase on a finite graph guarantees termination. Having only one stopping class is a separate numerical observation, not a consequence of finite termination or of the pivot construction. This is a walk in Bell-inequality space using stored scores; it does not run a new quantum optimization at each step or describe physical evolution of a state.

19  22:05–23:00  What the single ascent basin means physically
Separate three claims. First, exact support and rank checks can certify a specific facet, its ridge, and a pivot neighbor. Full closure then certifies enumeration, under the hypotheses on page nine. Second, on each analyzed recorded graph, the greedy rule has one stopping class and one basin. This is an exhaustive numerical graph result for that snapshot, not a theorem about undiscovered facets or exact quantum optima.

Third, there is a precise physical interpretation of the heights. Let \(P\) be an attained violating strategy for facet \(F\), with \(\widehat\beta_{Q,F}<\beta_{C,F}<0\), and \(P_{\mathrm{uni}}\) the uniform behavior; take \(0\leq v\leq1\). With \(I_F(P_{\mathrm{uni}})=0\), \(I_F(P_v)=v\widehat\beta_{Q,F}\). Since \(\beta_{C,F}<0\), strict violation is equivalent to \(v>1/\widehat\Delta_F\); equality is the local-bound threshold. An increasing recorded height therefore means lower demonstrated critical visibility, using a different independently optimized Bell test at each graph node.

This search path does not evolve a physical state toward an attractor. Nor does a single basin establish genuine four-party entanglement: lifted CHSH can reach its maximum with one entangled pair and unentangled spectators. The Mermin–Klyshko endpoint is a different resource example, but identifying that endpoint does not certify a resource property of every starting facet. Uniform-behavior noise should not automatically be identified with density-matrix white noise for arbitrary measurements.

The useful research implication is that adjacency may organize the search for robust Bell tests in these libraries. The later resource question adds a new constraint absent here: neighboring endpoints must use the same state and measurement tuple. This requires valid bounds covering every bipartition; SDP offers a practical route to obtain such certificates.

33  BACKUP  What exactly does the resource gap exclude?
