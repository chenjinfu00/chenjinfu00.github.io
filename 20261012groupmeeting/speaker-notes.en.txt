06  4:55–6:30  One pivot rotates a supporting inequality
Use the v0 minimizing convention. The slack is \(I_F(v)-\beta_C\geq0\). The child support \(J\) is nonnegative on \(F\) and vanishes on its ridge. Extend it so that some outside vertex has \(J(v)<0\). Increasing \(t\) preserves the ridge and consumes outside slack. The minimum slack-to-direction ratio is the largest valid step. The new tight face has dimension \(D-1\). Recenter the uniform behavior before comparing violation ratios.

23  26:30–27:55  The certificate covers all seven bipartitions
Quantum biseparability is the convex hull of quantum product models across all seven cuts, so a linear bound for every cut also covers their mixtures. Four singleton cuts reduce to CHSH. Two crossed pair cuts have score at most one. The remaining cut \(AB|CD\) permits two Bell pairs. A nine-word NPA matrix constrains each block’s CHSH score jointly with its flag probability. Sixteen residual-corrected rational Gram certificates validate midpoint bounds for all four sign choices. For negative-negative parity, \(q+(2/3)p_-\leq5/3\) has an exact SOS and yields the all-weight BS bound. A CHSH-maximizing Bell pair with a deterministic opposite block attains the larger-weight branch, making the bound exact. The seven cuts use six analytic bounds and one nontrivial SDP structure, not seven separate SDP solves.

32  BACKUP  Many-body symmetry can keep SDP blocks small
To test computational scaling, we now switch to a separate permutation-invariant quantum-bound benchmark. Extending R5 resource objectives with their surviving symmetry remains the next step. This closes the loop to PIBell. When the Bell objective and constraints retain full permutation symmetry, SDPBell assembles Hecke overlap blocks whose dimensions stay fixed at a chosen hierarchy level in the stable particle-number range. Level two has blocks 8, 8, 3, 1; level three has 18, 24, 13, 7, 4, 2. Particle number changes coefficients rather than those block sizes. At 500 particles, the PI two-body benchmark has attained value 1.213133491 and a level-three numerical upper estimate 1.213133634, a gap about 1.43 times ten to the minus seven. This is a numerical demonstration of tractability, not a rational exact-optimum certificate. A generic pivot pair is asymmetric: only the stabilizer preserved by its objective and constraints can be used. The proposed next step is therefore resource-specific finite-deficit optimization with the symmetry that actually survives.

33  BACKUP  What exactly does the resource gap exclude?
The resource benchmark is the set of quantum correlations generated by states separable across at least one bipartition, with quantum measurements inside each group, and then arbitrary convex mixing across cuts. We compare the unrestricted quantum support with that actual all-cut support. An observed score above the latter certifies genuine multipartite entanglement under the Bell assumptions. This is not a Svetlichny-type comparison with unrestricted nonsignaling correlations inside each group. Conversely, if the supports coincide, there is a biseparable realization attaining the optimum, but entangled maximizers may also exist. This distinction is especially important for the six zero-gap ridge orbits. The weighted scalar is generally not a facet, although its local exposed face is the shared intersection of the endpoint facets.

34  BACKUP  Which symmetry survives the weighted Bell target?
Symmetry reduction must preserve the optimization problem. The PI Hecke construction is appropriate when the objective and all constraints retain the full permutation symmetry. A generic asymmetric pivot pair does not satisfy that condition, so imposing full permutation invariance would remove valid strategies or change the objective. Its actual stabilizer may still reduce the moment matrix. The maintained three-outcome spin-one pilot is a finite-group prototype; its outer relaxation is not restricted to qutrit local dimension, even though the attainable strategy uses qutrits. Likewise, the local draft formulates Lindblad steady-state moment constraints, while numerical PI-Lindblad implementations are still a next target. These are useful development directions, but they must not be presented as completed benchmarks or exact finite-size optima.
